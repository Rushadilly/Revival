Revival: Joseph Vs. Aliens

Short story #4 of the Revival series! This one starts a little strange. Bare with me! I haven't jumped the shark!
…
Yet…

👽 Aliens

A young man walked down the street. With a smile on his face he trotted down city lanes passing storefronts and parallel parked cars without a care in the world.

But then, the sky ripped open.

With a whizz, a whirr, and the sort of theatrical entrance normally reserved for magicians, saucer-shaped UFOs swooped in overhead, their chrome sides gleaming with smug alien superiority.

Beams of light stabbed the pavement. People screamed and scattered like marbles spilled across tile. A hotdog cart tipped over, sending sausages rolling in every direction.

The young man dove behind the cover of a parked car. He was looking around, assessing the chaos unraveling before him when he heard a familiar voice.

“Joseph! Over here!” his father hollered.

He was out in the middle of the road — completely exposed — like nothing was going on.

He waved Joseph over. Joseph dashed from cover to cover, dodging alien beams that nearly atomized him, all the while his Dad just waited there for him.

Finally, Joseph reached him and cried out:

“Dad! What on earth is happening!?”

His father, calm as a man in slippers, announced,

“Son, I’ve been fighting these aliens for years. They think they’re clever with their abductions and their beams of light, but I’ve been outwitting them since before you could walk.”

Joseph blinked. “Y-You’ve been… at war with aliens?”

“Yes,” his father said matter-of-factly, tugging at his sweater vest as if preparing for a lecture.

“They’ve been stealing people—snatching them up and whisking them to their dreadful home world.”

Joseph’s mouth hung open. He couldn't believe what he was hearing come from his father.

“Which brings me to you, my son,” his father continued, eyes twinkling.
“You're going to help me beat them.”

“How could I possibly do that!?”

“You'll let yourself be taken up by the aliens into their home world, but you will have a powerful weapon smuggled on you.”

Joseph bounced with excitement.

“Ooh! Espionage!”

The Father chuckled at his son's enthusiasm.

“Let's not waste a moment, shall we? Here is your weapon!”

His father handed him a small yellow gun-shaped object with a bubbly liquid bottle sticking out of it.

Joseph turned it over in his hand.

“Is this a bubble gun?”

“Don’t you worry. It’s super effective!”

Joseph stared at his father like he’s crazy — and his father stared back, with a smile as if to say ‘You’ll see.’

🪐 Home-world

Just then, a UFO tilted, whirring loudly, and a tractor beam shimmered down onto the asphalt.

His father clapped him on the back.

“Now’s your chance, son! Go forth, be brave!”

With Joseph's mission before him, he darted forth into the light. The beam yanked him upward — and in a moment, he vanished into the ship.

Inside, he was met with little green men — short, round-headed, bug-eyed creatures who looked like they’d escaped from a toy store.

They circled him, poking at him from every angle.

“This one,” one alien croaked.

“Yes, this one,” another agreed.

“He will be our successor!” they squeaked in eerie unison.

Joseph looked around him, trying to understand. That was the last thing he expected to hear.

“W-what?”

Instead of responding, they tossed him onto a foreign world and clamped him in chains beside a crowd of weary figures — little grey men, eyes wide and mournful.

Joseph staggered back. He turned — and there were more. He tried to escape, but they were everywhere.

Then he heard that same familiar voice behind him.

“Don’t be alarmed.”

Joseph turned. “Dad!? How are you here!? I thought you were back on Earth!”

His father simply chuckled.

“The aliens think they can keep me out, but they can’t.”

Joseph glared at his father, no more answers than before.

The grey men shuffled closer, rattling their chains. Joseph tensed, but his father whispered,

“Don’t fear them, my son. They are the abducted humans. Use what I gave you.”

Joseph glanced back at his father and shook his head at the absurdity of it all.

He took out the bubble gun and aimed it — but his father interrupted.

“One more thing. The enemy will do everything in their power to stop you. Don’t let them intimidate you into backing down.”

“Ok.”

Joseph squeezed the trigger.

With a quick pshh, glistening bubbles floated out, fluttering onto the metal shackles.

With a hiss of smoke, the bubbles dissolved the chains.

Joseph and the grey men gasped in unison. One began clapping, another wept into his oversized alien hands.

But there was no time for celebration.

A blood-curdling screech pierced Joseph’s ears. His neck hairs stood on end.

He turned to see this… thing.

Something dark and spindly that towered high above him.

Its appendages looked like freakishly long bones and its mouth was crowded with blades for teeth — more than could be counted.

It shrieked again and started hunting him down, stepping over the little grey men faster than Joseph could run.

Joseph stumbled back and fell, unable to look away. Try as he might, there was nothing he could do to escape.

Twenty yards. Fifteen. Ten. Five…

Too close.

The last thing he saw were the teeth — endless rows of knives coming for his face—

Then everything went black…

…

🔆 Light

Suddenly, a bright light shone on Joseph's face.

“Wake up! Wake up! The sun got up and so should you!” said some loud, bubbly voice.

Joseph’s eyes wanted to remain clamped shut, but he forcibly blinked them open.

He looked around. All was dark. He couldn't tell where he was — only the blinding light coming from what looked like curtains.

Maybe he’d recognize something if he could see what was out there.

He dragged himself forward. The light burned as it filled more of his vision. It became too intense, so he shielded his eyes.

Slowly, his eyes adjusted… and he saw that his hand was — not a hand.

“Oh…” he thought.

He remembered where he was now. He had thought he was home; he had thought he was human — but that was just a fantasy.

The hand he held in front of his face was a paw.

He had hoped that this was the dream, but that hope was fading.

He was an Eevee.

And no amount of wishing was going to change that.

Joseph got up with a stretch. He was back at camp with the same Pokémon who had found him before.

Walking away from his tent was the Pikachu — probably the one who woke him.

The Pikachu joined Snout the Lucario, who sat by a lit fire, eating.

Joseph inhaled the fresh air — then immediately exhaled like a fly had gone up his nose.

The scent of roasted chicken flooded his senses stronger than ever before.

What did they do to the camp!?

Did they litter the entire place with cooked chicken!?

Or… more likely, his sense of smell was just stronger now as an Eevee.

Joseph hadn’t thought about food till now — but his stomach had. It felt like it had been days.

He was starving.

Still, too embarrassed to ask for food, he thought maybe if he sat with them long enough they’d have pity and feed him.

That, or he’d go hungry.

👊 Confrontation

Joseph left his tent and walked toward them, but Fight the Tyrogue stepped in front of him, blocking his path.

“Hey there! Have fun last night?” he said with a toothy grin.

“Uh, yeah… I guess…”

“Is that so? Good for you. It wasn’t so fun for me, though.”

Fight’s voice was laced with thick, fake enthusiasm. He sounded like he was talking down to a child.

“You see, Eli relies on me to keep track of everything. So when the new guy suddenly disappears and I can’t tell him where he is, that looks really bad on me.”

Joseph took a deep breath, trying not to show that it was getting to him.

The smell rolling off Fight hit him next — harsh and bitter, like a warning to back down.

His fur prickled before he could stop it.

“So next time you decide to go explore and get lost—” Fight’s smile dropped.
“—don’t.”

“…Ok…”

Fight glared down at him, scoffed, and walked away.

Joseph exhaled slowly, pretending that didn’t just happen, then joined the others by the fire.

🍗 Chicken

“There you are, sleepyhead! You sleep like a Snorlax,” the Pikachu piped up.

“Chew! Don’t be rude!” snapped Snout.

“Sorry.”

Joseph chuckled awkwardly. “Heh—uhm… hi.”

He sat beside them, fidgeting his paws together. Every movement felt wrong, even his body language felt foreign.

“Good morning,” said Snout.
“Are you hungry?”

“Uh… yeah, kinda…” Oh thank God, he thought.

Snout nodded and turned toward a bundle of wrapped leaves near the fire.

“You’re gonna love it! Snout’s the best cook!” Chew said.

“Oh, hush,” Snout replied, but her faint smile betrayed the compliment’s truth.

She shifted the coals, pulled out a flat stone heating beneath them.

On it sizzled strips of something savory, smoky scent thick in the air.

The aroma hit Joseph like a wave. His nose twitched — everything smelled so strong.

Snout peeled open a leaf bundle. Inside lay a roasted cut of meat, browned and steaming. She placed it in front of Joseph.

“Eat,” she said gently.

Joseph blinked. It looked exactly like a chicken leg — too perfect, too familiar.

But that couldn’t be right… There weren’t supposed to be chickens here.

So what was this?

The others were watching him.

He laughed nervously, picked up the leg. The smell was incredible — smoky, rich, layered with spice.

It wasn’t feral hunger, just too vivid.

He took a small bite. The flavor burst open — buttery, hot, impossibly real.

“Why are you eating your leg like that?” Fight asked, smirking.
“You look like you’re afraid to hurt it.”

Joseph froze mid-chew. Maybe he was eating weird. He took a bigger bite, forcing normalcy.

“Leave him alone, Fight,” Snout said.

“Hey, do you like it?” Chew asked.

All eyes were on him again.

“Y-yeah. It’s pretty good. Thanks.”

His voice cracked a little.

“See? Told you! Snout’s the best cook in the whole region,” Chew grinned.

“Hardly,” Snout muttered, smiling anyway.

Joseph looked back down at the leg. The firelight gleamed off the browned skin.

The smell tugged at him again — richer than anything he’d ever known as a human.

He took another slow bite, but the thought still lingered:

What kind of meat is this, really?

⚔️ Mission

Joseph tried to focus on eating, but his mind wouldn’t stop racing.

Every bite carried two flavors — the savory meat, and the sour tang of questions.

Where did this meat come from? What was it?

Is there a family of Pidgeys missing their father right now?

Before he could spiral further, the camp hushed.

Eli, the Ninetales, approached.

“Good,” he said, voice steady as stone.
“You’re all awake.”

“Even our newcomer.”

Joseph froze mid-bite.


“Uh… hi.”

“Did you enjoy your walk last night?”

Fight’s eyes widened.

Joseph stammered, “I- uh- Yeah… It- it was nice…”

“Very good.”

Eli turned to the others.

“Snout, Fight. You two will proceed hunting the bounty from yesterday.”

Snout rose immediately. “Yes, Eli,” she said, sounding almost excited.

Fight stretched, joining her.

“One more thing,” Eli added.
“Capture, not kill. The Guild will want the thief alive.”

“Alive. Got it,” Snout grinned.

“I’ll make sure she doesn’t get carried away, sir,” Fight said.

“That will be sufficient.”

“Anything for me?” Chew piped up.

“You’ll remain here. Keep the camp watched.”

“On it!”

Joseph looked around, waiting for orders.

“Uh—what about me? What do I do?”

Eli looked back with a side glance.

“…Stand there and look pretty.”

And he walked off.

🎲 Games

Joseph’s ear twitched.

“…Huh?…”

Was that a joke? A test?

Chew giggled. “Well, you are kinda fluffy.”

Joseph looked down at the ridiculous plumage around his neck.

“Yeah, you’re not wrong…”

Chew’s tail sparked.

“So, uh… you wanna play a game?”

“Uh… sure, I guess?”

“It’s kinda like tag! We call it Static Chase. You run, I chase. If you reach the firepit first, you win. If I zap you, I win.”

Joseph frowned.

“That sounds rigged. You can shoot lightning, can’t you?”

“Only a little! Promise I won’t fry you — just a tickle zap.”

“A tickle zap? Is that supposed to sound fun?”

“Better than sitting around looking gloomy! Besides, you’re new — I gotta see if you’re quick enough to keep up.”

Joseph scoffed, tossing his head back.

“Gah, fine.”

What do you think that “chicken” was anyway?
