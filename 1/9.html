Revival: Breaking Point (1/3)

I hope you’ve been lifting, because issue #9 of Revival gets heavy. 🏋️‍♂️
Longing. Dread. Desperation. Rebellion. Regret. Betrayal.
I’ll say little more — you’ll see for yourself.

I wonder, though… would you be able see Joseph the same way again?

🌅 New Day

The camp was already stirring when Joseph woke.
Morning light pressed through the seams of his tent, warm and steady, chasing away the pale blue of dawn.

He blinked and flexed his paws — still paws. He’d half-expected to see fingers again, half-expected the dream to end.

It hadn’t.

The novelty had provided some intrigue at first, but that was beginning to dull, leaving only an ache buried beneath it.

Then the thought surfaced — the meeting.
He’d promised Soot he’d preach with her this evening.
The idea pressed against the back of his mind like a weight he couldn’t ignore.

He dreaded it, though the relief that it wasn’t yet let him breathe again.

Outside, voices and the smell of breakfast drifted through the air.

Snout clattered pots near the fire. Chew and Fight’s shouts echoed faintly from the fields beyond — one laughing, the other deadly serious.

Joseph watched them from a distance, feeling that same quiet warmth and the same hollow space just behind it.

These were his new companions, his family, or so he tried to tell himself.
But lately that word felt thinner.

The day passed in fragments —
Helping Snout fetch water.
Mending a torn satchel.
Watching the sun crawl higher until the air shimmered with heat.

Every task filled the silence just enough to keep the thoughts at bay — the ones of home, of Soot, of the ache that never really left — until they came again anyway.

⛰️ Outlook

By afternoon he’d found a shaded ridge overlooking the valley.
The grass was warm beneath him; the wind carried scents he didn’t recognize.

For a long while he simply sat, caught between two worlds: one of fur and sunlight, one of skin and home.

He remembered his brother’s grin — the way he’d toss out some outrageous comment just to watch everyone’s faces twist in disbelief.
He remembered the quiet talks with his father about science and tech advancements.
His mother’s silent care — how she showed her love in everything she did.
And his sister — stern, protective, grounding him when the world felt too big.

He didn’t realize how much he missed them until a tightness rose in his chest.

The wonder was gone.
Only the distance remained.

By the time the sun dipped low, the sky bruised into gold and violet.
He drew a breath, steeling himself. Evening meant the meeting — the one he’d promised.

Soot would be waiting.

And so, as the shadows lengthened and the last light bled from the valley, Joseph made his way toward the city’s edge — toward the alley where darkness gathered early.

👨‍👩‍👦 Familiar Calling

Voices mingled with the clatter of carts and the smell of roasted meat. The streets seemed busier than usual — last-minute shoppers before the stalls closed, maybe.

He turned off the main road into a narrow lane until the noise began to fade.
The alley ahead matched Soot’s description: dim, cramped, tucked between two leaning buildings.

But she wasn’t there.

He waited a minute, then walked a short loop, checking for another turn he might’ve missed. The shadows deepened. The sound of the crowd dulled to a distant hum.

Maybe he’d arrived too early.
Or too late.
He couldn’t tell anymore without a watch, and in the evening was far too vague for his liking.

Leaning against the cold stone, Joseph let his thoughts drift — back home.
Back to laughter and warmth and familiar voices.

He pictured his brother’s chaos, his father’s quiet talks, his mother’s touch, his sister’s steady presence.
The ache behind his ribs pulsed with every image.

A voice reached him from the street, one he thought he recognized.

“Jo—”

His heart skipped. The rest filled itself in: Joseph!

He spun toward the sound, chest tight with impossible hope.

“Jolt! Come here, hun!”

The air left him. Not his mother. Just another mon.

Of course it wasn’t her. It could never be her.
But oh, how he wanted it to be.

💔 Cracks

The crowd’s noise faded — swallowed by the narrow walls.
The world went on without him — laughter, calls, the soft rhythm of life — and he stood apart from it, as if behind glass.

Pressure swelled in his chest until it throbbed in his throat.
He tried to breathe it away, to bury it like he'd done before, but the air hitched halfway up his lungs.

He pressed a paw to his chest, heartbeat shaking through his body.

Another breath — shallow, quick.
Then another, shaking.

No, you can’t cry! Not here! Not now!

But the images came anyway.
His mother’s fading voice.
His father’s hand.
His sister’s laughter.

Each memory cut deeper until his whole chest ached with wanting.

A sound escaped him — half gasp, half sob — too quiet to echo but enough to shame him.

He forced the air out through his mouth, slow and silent, until his lungs burned.
Then again, until the shaking eased just enough to stand still.

He’d learned how to do this — to fold the pain into smaller and smaller spaces until it fit somewhere deep inside, where no one else could see.

After a few more breaths it was back under control — barely.

He stayed like that for a while, breathing quietly in the dark.

He was wiping the last of the tears from his face when a voice broke through the quiet behind him.

“Joseph?”

He startled — the sound too close, too sudden. He turned his head quickly, blinking back the wet in his eyes and forcing his breath to steady.

“H—hey, Soot.” he said, trying to sound friendly.

📦 Preparations

The Umbreon gave him a look. Not sharp — just… assessing.

“You look like you lost a fight with the wind.”

A choked sound escaped him — almost a laugh.

“Yeah, something like that.”

“You okay?”

“I’m fine.”

He straightened, quick to deflect.

“So, about what you said last night — about sharing salvation. What did you have in mind?”

“You’re the one who’s done this before, not me,” Soot said.

Joseph sighed.

Not really.

He’d never talked to someone with the express purpose of sharing the gospel.
But no one else here knew it like he did, making him the defacto expert.

“I guess I could… climb onto something and talk? Like, preach. But no one’s going to listen to me.”

“Then start with something they will listen to,” she said.
“Tell them who you are — the Eevee who didn’t bow.”

He cringed. “Does… everyone know about that?”

“Word gets around fast when you stand out like you did,” she said, almost smirking. Then, softer:
“Use it. They’ll stop to stare — make them stay to listen.”

He searched her face, but she was already glancing toward the street, as if to hide the faint trace of encouragement behind her usual cool.

“Go on,” she said.
“Before you talk yourself out of it.”

Joseph swallowed, heart pounding, and stepped out from the dim alley into the glow of the evening street.

He glanced back once — but Soot was gone.

The chatter of the marketplace dulled to a distant hum as he approached an empty crate by a vendor’s stall. He climbed up, heart pounding so loud it nearly drowned out the world.

Then, quietly—

“My name’s Joseph… I’m the Eevee who wouldn’t bow.”

🎙️ Preaching

The words sounded steadier than he felt. His chest trembled, but he forced his posture tall — chin up, voice firm, the way a leader might stand.

The title Eevee who wouldn’t bow drew glances.
Heads turned — whispers followed.

“I didn’t bow because I don’t serve that god,” he said.
“When the strike came, the wind blew — as a sign — and I was spared. I didn’t save myself. I couldn’t have.”

His voice rose:

“It was God who did it. He is the one who sees the lowly. The one who saves.”

The crowd began to form — small at first, but growing.

Soot emerged from the alley, silent as smoke.
She didn’t move closer, choosing instead to linger in the shadow just beyond the crowd’s fringe.

She watched him for a moment, surprised by how composed he looked. For all his quiet awkwardness, he seemed born for this.

She moved her attention to the crowd, scanning faces until one froze her in place.

Fang.

The Mightyena’s dark fur caught the low glow of the sun. He stood among the listeners, head slightly lowered, his gaze fixed on Joseph.

For a heartbeat, Soot almost smiled.
He came.
Maybe he’d find the same peace she had. Maybe he'd understand.

But then she saw the stiffness in his jaw, the suspicion in his stare. He wasn’t moved — he was measuring.

Joseph’s voice carried on, but the rhythm faltered.
The air thickened around him.

Every time someone’s eyes locked onto his, he felt smaller, like the street itself was folding in on him.
His pulse raced, his words tangled.

“I… I just want you to know there’s—”

Then nothing.

The rest vanished, and couldn’t find it again.
His heart raced, and for a moment, every eye was on him.

“…Excuse me a moment.”

He climbed down from the crate, brushing past the crowd.
They parted around him like ripples, muttering under their breath.

He didn’t remember moving.
Sound warped — a low hum, a distant murmur.
His chest burned but he couldn’t feel it, couldn’t think past the fog crowding his head.
Only one thought broke through, quiet but absolute:

Find Soot.

Something moved in the alley’s shadow.
His mind stumbled over the thought before a single word broke through.

“Soot?”

But she turned — and bolted.

🧱 Resistance

Joseph followed without thinking, shouldering past startled onlookers until he found her in the alley, half-hidden behind a stack of crates.

“W-what was that about?" he asked, breathless.
"Why’d you run?”

She didn’t meet his eyes.

“You shouldn’t be here with me. They can’t see us together.”

“What? What do you mean?”

“You’ll be labeled a sympathizer,” she hissed.
“A dark-type lover. They’ll hate you like they hate me.”

Joseph stared at her, the noise of the marketplace dull and distant.

"But—"

He almost started to argue, then caught himself with a tired scoff.

“Forget it. Soot, I don’t want to do this anymore.”

Her eyes flicked up, sharp.

“What? No! You can’t stop now. They were listening.”

He shook his head. “No, they were staring.”

“Same thing,” she shot back.
“You got through to them — to me. You have to show it to them too.”

He let out a low groan, dragging a paw down his face.

“Soot…”

She stepped closer, her voice dropping.

“Just try one more time. Isn’t this what God would want?”

He hesitated, but reluctantly, he nodded.

“Good,” she said softly, giving his shoulder a gentle nudge.
“Go on.”

Joseph exhaled through his nose as he walked back toward the street.

"I want to go home." he muttered under his breath.

💨 Pressure

The crowd hadn’t fully dispersed — a few lingered, curious, murmuring.
He climbed back onto the crate, forcing his breathing steady.
His paws shook, so he locked them in place, pretending they didn’t.
The preacher’s mask slid back on — the one with the calm voice and the faint, knowing smile.

“My name’s Joseph,” he began again.


“I’m not here to argue or to shame anyone. I just want you to know…”

But the air had changed.
A low voice cut through the murmur:

“Saved by the wind, huh?”

Joseph’s gaze drifted to the edge of the circle.
Fang stood there, head lowered, eyes gleaming.

“You really expect anyone to believe that?” the Mightyena growled.
“That your god blew through just for you? Sounds like a fancy way to call yourself special.”

A few others muttered in agreement. The crowd’s earlier curiosity shifted — the edges sharpened.

Joseph froze, his throat dry.
He could feel Soot somewhere in the shadows, watching.

Stay calm, he told himself, straightening, forcing a steady tone.
If he looked confident, maybe they’d listen.
Maybe he’d believe it too.

Fang stepped closer.

“Tell me, Eevee. Think your god’s gonna send another breeze to save you now?”

Soot’s tail flicked with impatience as she watched the scene shift.

He’s fine. She thought. He can handle his.

But as Fang’s voice grew louder, that certainty wavered.
Joseph's ears were pinned flat now. His shoulders had sunk.

The tension crawling up her spine whispered the truth — maybe he wasn’t fine at all.

Come on Joseph…

🔥 Burning

Joseph stood on the crate, Frozen.
As the jeers closed in his thoughts spun cynical.

He didn't even want to be here.
He’d only climbed up there because Soot had asked. She put him up to this, this was her idea.

He shook his head. No, don’t think that.

He opened his mouth, searching for something to say — anything — but all that came out were stammers.

“I—I just—”

Fang cut him off with a snarl.

“What? Lose your bark, preacher?”

Joseph’s jaw trembled.
He wanted to tell them to shut up — to scream it — but he bit his tongue until it hurt.
The crowd’s laughter turned into a single, choking blur of noise.

Every heartbeat rattled through his chest like thunder. He couldn’t think — couldn’t breathe.
The mask was starting to crack, and he had nothing else to hide behind.

Pressure built in his chest again like it did in the alley, but this time it burned — not from panic, but from something heavier, hotter, twisting under his ribs.

He couldn’t go home.
He couldn’t stop.
And now he was stuck preaching, because Soot had pushed him here.
This is her fault.

Joseph shook his head hard.
No! Stop it! Stop thinking like that!

Fang’s voice rose again, rough with contempt.

“You think your God cares about the likes of us?” Fang barked.
“He’s just like the rest of you — you only care about the clean!”

➿ Spiral

Joseph’s heart pounded beneath the eyes of the crowd. Every gaze was a weight, every whisper a nail pressing into his chest.

He wanted to stop — to vanish — but Soot’s voice still echoed in his head:
“You can’t stop now.”

He couldn’t keep doing this. Couldn’t keep performing a faith he didn’t feel. Couldn’t keep pretending to be whole.

Then, something inside him snapped — quiet, like a string drawn too tight.

Fine. If they wanted to hate him, let them.
If Soot wanted him to preach, then he’d make sure neither of them could ever do it again.

He’d only need to show them the truth.

He straightened, voice sharp and shaking.

“Fine! You want to see what my God does to the shamed and the outcast?”

A ripple spread through the crowd — surprise, confusion, curiosity.
Even Fang fell silent.

Soot froze. That sounded like… her…

“What are you doing…” she whispered under her breath.

Joseph jumped down from the crate, dust fleeing where he landed.

He started toward the alley, shouldering through fur and bodies, each touch lighting his skin with a feverish heat.
His thoughts spiraled faster — reckless, deliberate.

They’d see him with her.
They’d think the worst.
And then they’d never listen to him again.

He could almost laugh at how simple it was.

She wanted a sermon, he thought, so I’ll give her one.

Soot’s dark shape caught the fading light. She turned at the sound of his steps — and froze.
No!Not now! Not here! Not in front of them!_

“Joseph?” she whispered, panic coiling tight in her throat.

He kept coming, a strange grin flickering across his face.

Her pulse jumped.

“Go back,” she hissed.
“You don’t understand—”

But he kept walking. Her heart slammed against her ribs.
He was going to ruin himself.

Soot’s breath caught. She didn’t think — she just acted.
Her voice rang out, sharp and desperate:

“Get away from me, you creep!”

The marketplace went still.

Joseph blinked. The words hit like a slap — louder, harsher than she’d ever sounded before.

But then something broke open in him, wild and bitter.

That was it. That was the confirmation he needed.
Even Soot didn’t want him near.

Perfect, he thought, as if he wanted to believe it.

He let the grin spread, crooked and empty.

“That’s not very nice,” he said softly.

Soot’s breath hitched.

“Get away, or I’ll—”

“I’d like to see you try.”

His tone was half-taunt, half-laugh, and every word felt like tearing off a piece of himself.

The whispers started.
The crowd pressed closer.
Soot’s stomach turned to ice.

“Stop it!” she shouted, voice cracking.

She turned and ran.

Joseph laughed after her — wild, breathless, wrong.
For the first time, he wasn’t pretending.
He wasn't preaching
He wasn’t the Eevee who wouldn’t bow.

He was done.

He ran after her, letting the noise and heat of the market blur behind him.

🔪 Fangs

The laughter was still on his lips when the world flipped.

A heavy force yanked him back by the scruff — the ground disappeared as his hind legs flew forward.
His back hit the dirt hard, air slamming from his lungs.
Pain flashed white behind his eyes.

A heavy paw pressed into his chest, pinning him down.
He gasped, but the weight crushed every breath.

Fang loomed over him, fangs bared, eyes blazing.

“You touch her and I’ll rip you apart!”

Joseph dragged in shallow breaths, each one scraping against the weight crushing his ribs.
His body screamed for air he couldn’t seem to find. The edges of the world pulsed with heat and noise—his pulse, his panic, his pain.

For a heartbeat, he wanted to fight back — to scream — but then the shame hit him all at once.

Of course.
This was what he wanted, wasn’t it?
To be hated?
To be nothing?

He went still beneath Fang’s paw.
Barely able to breath, he felt like he was drowning. Whatever happened next, he accepted it.
He deserved it.

Then Soot’s voice broke through the chaos.

“Fang!” she cried with real anger.
“Let him go!”

Fang hesitated. His eyes flicked toward her, confusion flashing just long enough for his weight to ease.

The air rushed back into Joseph’s chest in a ragged gasp — burning, raw, alive.
He coughed, every breath stabbing through his ribs as if his body couldn’t decide whether to keep him living.

Instinct took over.

He twisted free, stumbling until his paws found traction.

He ran.

From the market.
From her.
From everything.

He’d done it.
He Destroyed himself.
Now she’d never ask again.
Now she’d never want him again.

He felt sick to his stomach.
His heart felt like it'd burst.

He kept running until even the sound of everything vanished behind him.

🕳️ Fallout

Behind him, Soot stood frozen at the alley’s mouth, watching the space where he’d vanished. Her chest felt hollow, the noise of the market distant and cold.

Then her gaze snapped to Fang. Without a word, she lunged, clamping her teeth around his ear.

“Gah—! Hey—Soot! What—what the hell!?”

He yelped, stumbling as she dragged him back into the alley, away from the crowd's prying eyes.

When she finally let go, her voice came raw.

“What is wrong with you!?”

Fang staggered, clutching his ear.

“I thought he was coming for you! He—he looked crazy—”

“He was trying to help you!” she shouted, tears catching in her throat.
“Now he’s gone because of you!”

Her voice cracked on the last word. Fang shrank back, shame clouding his face.

“I’m sorry,” he murmured.
“I just wanted to keep you safe.”

Fang shrank back, shame clouding his face.

Soot turned away, trembling. The fight drained out of her all at once, leaving only the hollow ache beneath it.

She stared toward the alley’s mouth — the way he’d gone — hoping he’d turn back.

But he didn’t.

The echo of her own words—Get away from me!—rang in her ears until she couldn’t tell if she’d shouted them or dreamed them.

“He’ll never forgive me,” she whispered.

And the silence that followed felt like losing him all over again.

Around them, the marketplace noise crept back — laughter, footsteps, the sound of life moving on — while Soot stood frozen, afraid that somewhere in the dark, Joseph had already decided she was right.
