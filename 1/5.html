Revival: Static Chase

Short story #5 of the Revival series! A good ol' game of Static Chase. Tagging someone down with striking bolts of electricity. What could go wrong?

⚡ Chase

Sparks flashed left.

Joseph's heart pounded.

Bolts jumped right.

Breathing too fast. Slow it down.

The world blurred past at a speed too fast to be safe—or it at least felt that way because of his smaller size.

“Look out! I'm gonna getchya!” shouted Chew.

Joseph locked in on the campfire. Get to that and he will be spared getting shocked.

Another crack of light flashed at the edge of his vision.

He couldn't tell if the Pikachu was going easy on him or if he had poor aim.

Doesn't matter. Keep going. Keep running.

His lungs cried out for more oxygen.

He was tempted to start hyperventilating, but he knew that wouldn’t help.

His legs protested their abuse.

He’d never run like this before—at least, not as an Eevee.

He was surprised he even knew how to run in this body.

It’s like it came with its own muscle memory pre-installed.

Another bolt darted around him—close.

His fur stood on end.

The smell hit him: cold metal and cucumbers, sharp enough to sting his nose.

Almost there. Almost there. Keep pushing.

The tents whizzed past him when he realized he needed to slow down before crashing into the smoldering embers.

He dug his paws into the ground and tripped, nearly rolling into the fire pit.

The Pikachu sped toward him, still sparking.

“Wait! Wait! I reached the pit!” Joseph pleaded — but Chew already sent another bolt.

It hit.

His body seized as he fell back into the dirt.

🏆 Winning

Joseph’s limbs twitched as the charge faded, a burnt tang hanging in the air.

Chew skid to a stop, laughing.

“Haha! How was that? Having fun?”

Joseph moaned, pushing the ground away from him.

“You said I'd win if I got to the campfire.” he said with his glare fixed on the Pikachu.

Chew tilted his head, ears flicking like he hadn’t considered Joseph’s complaint worth much weight.

“Yeah, yeah, I did say that. But—”

He bounded forward and tapped Joseph’s forehead with a paw.

“—I also said if I zapped you, I win.”

Joseph shook Chew's hand from his head.

“You can’t just change the rules!”

“I didn’t change the rules. You just didn’t listen.”

Joseph’s fur bristled, heat rising in his chest.

“That’s not fair! I literally made it to the pit. You saw me!”

He bared his teeth.

Chew just wiggled his tail smugly, sparks still dancing across his cheeks.

“You made it, yeah—but you didn’t win. There’s a difference.”

💥 Losing

Joseph's eyes narrowed as he internalized an exasperated sigh—

the kind that would’ve been silent when he was human,

but in this body, it escaped differently—low, guttural.

A growl.

The sound startled even him.

His ears flicked back. Eyes darted down.

He didn’t mean that. He didn’t even realize he could do that.

He wasn’t that invested in the stupid game—was he?

Chew’s reaction was immediate.

His smug grin flickered and vanished.

Ears twitched back, shoulders tensed.

Sparks that moments ago had been playful fizzled out.

For a heartbeat, his eyes widened with something raw—fear.

Joseph blinked.

“…Chew?”

But as quick as it appeared, the expression was gone.

Chew snapped his posture straighter, tail giving a too-fast wag.

“Heh. Didn’t know you had that in you,”

his voice pitched a little too high.

He grinned again, but it was tight around the edges.

“I—I didn’t mean to,” Joseph stammered, voice thin.
“It was an accident.”

Chew gave a short laugh, crackling like static.

“Accident, yeah, sure. Happens all the time.”

He kicked at the dirt, not quite meeting Joseph’s eyes.

“Anyway, you, uh—you reached the pit, so… guess that’s a win, right?”

💫 Aftershock

Joseph’s stomach knotted.

This wasn’t what he wanted at all.

He hadn’t cared about winning.

He just didn’t want to get shocked.

But now Chew was backpedaling—tail twitching, sparks long gone.

Not proud, not teasing—placating.

And that felt worse.

“No, no, that’s not—”

Joseph’s ears pressed flat.

“I wasn’t— I didn’t want to scare you.”

He stepped closer—then froze when Chew shuffled back a half-step.

Barely noticeable, but enough to sting.

“I didn't want— I didn’t mean to… growl like that.”

His voice wavered, high-pitched in ways he still wasn’t used to.

“I’m not mad at you, okay?”

Chew snorted, a sharp puff of air.

“You sure sounded mad.”

Joseph winced.

“I just—”

He fumbled, paws kneading the dirt.

His thoughts scrambled for the right words, but what came out sounded half-broken.

“I’m not scary. I don’t want you to think that. I didn’t mean to scare you.”

Chew’s tail flicked—not smug this time, but restless.

His eyes narrowed, though not unkindly.

“You don’t gotta baby me. I can take a growl.”

Joseph’s chest tightened.

“I know, but… I don’t want to be that guy, alright? The one who… who makes you feel like you gotta give up or back down or…”

His voice thinned to a whisper.

“…or get smaller, just because of me.”

The words fell heavy between them.

He didn’t even know if they made sense out loud—he was just trying to untangle the knot inside himself.

But Chew tilted his head, blinking slowly.

The sparks on his cheeks dimmed to nothing.

For a long moment, neither of them spoke.

Joseph’s gaze fell, shame curling through him.

His “apology” felt clumsy, half-selfish—like he was saying it more for his own comfort than Chew’s.

And maybe he was.

He was terrified of the idea that he could be someone’s threat—even for a second.

“…You’re weird,” Chew finally muttered, but his voice had softened.

Joseph lifted his head, meeting Chew’s gaze again.

He didn’t have any words—but the faintest tug pulled at his core.

Joseph blinked, unsure how to take it—

—but Chew’s grin, faint but real this time, told him enough.

The air between them eased, if only a little.

Before either could say more, a voice rang out across camp.

📌 Errand

“Joseph! Chew!”

Eli’s deep, commanding tone carried without effort.

The Ninetales stood tall at the edge of the clearing, nine pale-gold tails fanning in the breeze.

“That's enough fun. I need you two to run to town for some supplies.”

Chew snapped to attention—the awkward tension gone, replaced by a spark of eagerness.

“Town run? Finally!”

He gave his fur a brisk shake, almost looking like his old self.

Joseph, still caught between relief and unease, only nodded.

His paws shifted in the dirt, gaze flicking from Chew’s grin to Eli’s steady presence.

He wasn’t sure if he was ready to face the town again

—but at least it gave him something to run toward, instead of away from.
