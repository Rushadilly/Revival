Revival: Ashes in the River (2/2)

Short story #8 of Revival!

🌑 Water's Edge

Soot stopped at the edge of the lake, her breath still uneven. The water shimmered faintly against her paws.

She and Joseph stood in silence, the lake breathing soft ripples against the shore. The moon hung low, turning the water into a sheet of silver glass. Every sound felt too loud — the chirr of insects, the whisper of grass, the faint creak of Joseph’s steps as he stopped beside her.

Soot didn’t look at him. She didn’t have to. She already knew what he was thinking.

Or so she told herself.

He must hate me for following him.
He must be embarrassed they saw him with me.
He must be wondering why he ever bothered.

The thoughts pressed heavy, until her chest felt tight. The reflection of his shape wavered beside hers in the dark water — two distorted outlines side by side, never quite touching.

“You okay?” Joseph asked finally. His voice came quiet, unsure.
“They didn’t hurt you, did they?”

The question caught her off guard. She had braced for anger, not this.

“I’m fine,” she said quickly, a little too sharp.
“I’ve had worse.”

He didn’t argue. Just looked up at her with that same worried frown that made her feel exposed.

She glanced away, tail twitching.

“Why’d you do that anyway? Back there, in the camp. Why risk yourself for me?”

Joseph hesitated, eyes drawn to the shifting light on the lake.

“Because…” He paused, almost testing the words.
“Because it was right.”

Soot blinked, thrown.

“Right?” she echoed, like it was a foreign word.

People didn’t help her because it was right. They helped for reward, for pity, or not at all. The idea that someone would help her because it should be done — that didn’t make sense.

Her voice softened. “You actually believe that?”

He nodded once, eyes still on the water.

“Yeah. I do.”

🌙 Greater Faith

The silence stretched again, gentler this time, before Soot broke it.

“I heard you wouldn’t bow,” she said.
“What was that about?”

“Oh, that…” Joseph’s expression soured.
“T-there was some religious parade,” he said.
“They wanted us all to bow. I couldn't do it.”

She tilted her head.

“You mean… you wouldn’t bow to Arceus?”

“No.” He glanced at her.
“It wasn’t Arceus. It was some other statue. But even if it had been Arceus…” He hesitated.
“God is so much greater than him.”

Soot frowned.

“Greater than Arceus?”

She almost laughed but stopped herself. No one said that kind of thing out loud — not if they wanted to keep peace.

“You’re either brave or stupid to say that.”

“Maybe,” Joseph said, a faint smile tugging at his mouth.

The answer made her study him again — really study him — as if searching for what kind of fool would talk that way.

“So you think your God saved you?” she asked.

“I know He did,” Joseph said.
“He looks after those who belong to Him.”

“So who is He?” she asked quietly.
“This… greater one.”

Joseph’s voice came out low, almost fragile — like someone speaking of something too precious to be casual about.

“He’s not another legend,” he said.
“Not a guardian or force of nature. He’s the One who made everything — the stars, the sea… you, me. All of it.”

Soot’s ears twitched.

“Sounds a lot like Arceus.”

Joseph shook his head softly.

“I don’t know much about Arceus… but the One I’m talking about—He cares for us, even when we don’t care back. He gives because He loves.”

Soot blinked at that. A god that gives instead of demands? That wasn't the world she knew.

🔪 Telling Scars

Joseph’s gaze went distant.

“When I was at my lowest, He saw me. Pulled me out of a place I didn’t think I’d escape.” His tone wavered, sincere.
“…He said He felt nothing but love for me… even after I went against Him.”

The words touched her like light brushing against something long frozen. It wasn’t persuasion. It was care — warm, unguarded, and undeserved. And that gentleness stirred something in her chest that had been asleep for years.

She swallowed hard.

“You think He’d do that for me? For an Umbreon?”

Joseph’s ears dipped.

“You say that like it’s something to be ashamed of.”

Her voice turned bitter.

“It is. You don’t turn into one unless you choose it. Unless you stay in the dark and… like it.”

She looked down at her reflection — yellow rings glowing faintly against the night.

“Everyone sees it as betrayal. That I wanted this.”

Joseph’s chest ached at the pain in her words. He thought of his own failures, the times he’d hated himself for what he did.
He wanted to tell her she was wrong — that she wasn’t beyond hope — but then the question struck him like a blade of doubt:
Can Pokémon even be saved?

He pushed the thought aside, breathing in the cool air.

“I don’t think He makes mistakes,” he said.

She shook her head, her voice cracking.

“You don’t understand. I made myself like this.”

Joseph scooted just a little closer.

“Then maybe,” he said,
“He wants to make something new out of you anyway.”

💧 Breakthrough

Soot’s breath hitched.

“You think He could love… me?”

For a moment, he felt it again — that doubt. Would God even love these creatures — these beings literally called monsters?
But then he felt something else — heat and cold at once, reaching into his core and making his fur stand on end. It was the same presence he’d felt the night he was saved — steady, alive.

“I— I think He already does.”

That broke her. Not anger, not shame — but something deeper. A longing that trembled on the edge of her voice.

“Then tell me how,” she whispered.
“Tell me how I can be His.”

Joseph froze. He hadn’t meant to bring her to this.

“Soot— I-I—”

His words stumbled over each other. He didn’t want to misrepresent Christ.
What if she was disappointed? What if he failed her?

“It—it’s not just a few words,” he said at last.
“It’s your whole life. Following Him means changing everything. Leaving the darkness behind.”

She looked down, then back up at him.

“Then I’ll change.”

He shook his head.

“It’s not easy. It’s not free — not really. It’ll cost you everything.”

He kept talking — every stipulation he could think of, as if trying to warn her away.

“I don’t care,” she said, voice breaking.
“I’m tired of the dark. I just… I want to be loved again.”

Her plea hung between them, trembling in the moonlight.

✨ Commitment

Joseph’s heart twisted. He looked at her — then down, toward the reflection of the heavens.

Any being that truly wants God this earnestly should at least have the chance to experience Him.

He drew a slow breath and whispered,

“Lord, if I’m wrong, then please forgive me. But I feel for her. Please save her.”

He lifted his eyes, doubt quieted.

“Alright, come here,” he said, stepping into the water.

Soot hesitated, eyes flicking from him to the mirror-like surface. The water was cold, and she didn’t understand what he meant to do — but something in his tone, gentle and unflinching, pulled her forward.

He’s not afraid of me, she thought. Maybe I don’t have to be either.

She followed. The water lapped cold around their paws.

“You trust me?” he asked softly.

“I want to.”

He nodded once.

“That’s enough.”

She didn’t ask what came next. She just watched him, trusting.

And then — something changed.
It wasn’t visible, not quite. But the still air seemed to breathe. The world leaned closer. It was as if the lake, the moon, and the very night had turned their attention to this one fragile exchange.

Then Love looked at Soot through Joseph’s eyes — older than light, fierce and tender, seeing not the shape she had become, but the soul underneath.

🕊️ His Stories

Joseph’s voice trembled, not from fear but awe.

“In the beginning was God. He created all kinds of life, and set apart one to reflect His image…”

He told the story quietly, water rippling between them.

When he finished, Soot’s eyes glimmered.

“He… died? For them?”

“Yes,” Joseph said.
“Now all we have to do to live is accept His sacrifice, set aside our old lords, and accept Him as our true Lord. We show it through baptism — both in water and in the Spirit.”

“You said He set apart one kind of life.”

“Yes?”

“What kind was it?”

“It— …It was humankind.”

Her ears drooped.

“So… not Umbreon, then.”

He looked at her — really looked.

“If you aren’t that kind, you look just like it.”

She huffed, forcing a smirk.

“Guess you need better eyes.”

Joseph smiled faintly but said nothing.

The reeds swayed around them, the air thick with the hum of evening.

🌊 Ashes in the Water

Soot’s smirk lingered a moment longer, but her eyes stayed on the water, where the ripples shimmered in the dying light.

“Do you really think He’d want something like me?”

“He already does.”

That answer struck her silent. The wind whispered through the reeds — steady, patient.

Then, almost a whisper:

“Then I want that. I want to be His.”

Joseph’s throat tightened.

“Then all that’s left,” he said gently,
“is to go under, and let everything else wash away.”

Soot hesitated. The lake felt endless. She looked at her reflection — dark rings glowing faintly — and a tremor of fear ran through her.

What if nothing changes? What if He doesn’t take me?

But Joseph’s calm didn’t waver. His eyes held peace.

He believes this. Maybe I can, too.

She nodded once. Took a breath. And plunged.

The water swallowed her whole. For an instant there was only silence — cool pressure, heartbeat, stillness. Then—light.

It wasn’t blinding. It was through her — warmth where cold had lived too long.

She rose.

Her gasp broke the surface like the first breath of morning. Water streamed from her coat in silver arcs. Her rings pulsed, alive.

Joseph blinked — then felt it too.
A stillness, vast and electric, pressing against him from the inside out. Peace like a current, flowing through his chest until the air itself seemed sacred.

He knew that Presence.
The Spirit was here.
He felt it rest on her, and then on him — a confirmation clear as breath.

She is Mine.

The words weren’t heard but understood. And every doubt he’d carried dissolved into peace.

When their eyes met, Soot’s shimmered — not just wet, but bright, as if she’d seen the same unseen thing.

For a long time, neither spoke. The world was quiet, washed clean.

🌌 Prince of Peace

The lake had gone still again. Ripples fanned outward from where Soot had stood, settling into glassy calm. She sat on the shore beside Joseph, damp fur clinging to her frame.

For a long while, neither spoke. Only the wind did — brushing over the reeds, curling through the treetops.

“It doesn’t feel cold anymore,” she said.
“It’s like… the dark can’t reach me.”

“That’s because it can’t,” Joseph said softly.
“This is the beginning of salvation — the turning point.”

Her eyes shimmered.

“That’s what it is, then. The peace I feel.”

“That’s Him — the Prince of Peace.”

She smiled faintly, gaze lingering on the water.

“I still can’t believe He’d do that,” she murmured. “For me.”

“He did it for everyone,” Joseph said gently.
“God wants everyone to be saved — every last one of us.”

Soot blinked. Everyone.

Her breath hitched, something unreadable flickering behind her eyes.

“So… what now?”

🔔 Conviction

Joseph hesitated.

“I-I don’t know. I’m just trying to keep my head down — trying not to be noticed.”

Soot turned toward him.

“You can’t just hide.”

“What do you mean?”

“If He’d take in someone like me, then others need to know too.”

Her voice wavered, then steadied.

“You don’t get something like that and keep it to yourself. You show it — you live like you mean it.”

He opened his mouth, but she kept going, energy catching like flame.

“If He would call me His, then maybe others could be His too. They need to hear that.”

“Soot…”

“No, listen.” She stood tall, tail lashing once behind her.
“If I could feel this — after everything — then anyone can.”

Joseph flinched. Figures. I baptize her, and she’s already preaching better than me.

He swallowed.

“I— I can’t.”

“Can’t what?”

“I just… I can’t stand in front of people like that.”
“I just… can’t.”

Joseph looked up at her.

“Maybe you could do it,” he offered weakly.
“You’ve got the fire for it.”

🌆 Resolution

She glared at him with a sharp, unwavering gaze that almost made him shrink back.

“…Except I’m an Umbreon, remember? They’d never listen. They’d turn away before I even said a word.”

Joseph’s face fell.
The spark in his eyes dimmed as her words sank in. He looked away, jaw tightening for a moment before he managed a faint nod.
There was nothing he could say.

Then Soot softened.

“You’re the one they’d hear. You know what this feels like. How can you keep it to yourself?”

He couldn’t answer. Every excuse rang hollow.

“You can’t hide this forever.”

He breathed out slowly.

“I know.”

The two sat in silence. Fireflies rose from the reeds, their light flickering gently across Soot’s rings — small golden echoes of the hope burning inside her.

And though Joseph’s stomach knotted at the thought, he knew she was right.

“Tomorrow,” she said quietly.
“We’ll go to town. They need to hear this.”

Joseph looked up at her, moonlight catching faintly on his fur. He wanted to protest — to delay — but the look in her eyes left no room.

He exhaled a long, tired sigh.

“Alright,” he said.
“Tomorrow.”
