Issue #7 of Revival, where we take a darker turn to a world where grime grows, the shadows play, and ashes scatter. A fitting place for one called Soot.

---

## Night 🌖

The night draped itself across the land like a quiet veil.

Stars twinkled above, pale and distant, their cold light scattered on the dusty road below.

The only sound was the soft rhythm of paws pressing into the dirt — steady and deliberate.

**An Umbreon walked alone.**

Her rings gave off a faint, pulsing glow — dim enough not to draw eyes.

The moonlight slid across her sleek fur like oil over glass.

Her golden eyes, dulled by exhaustion, barely blinked as she turned a corner and stepped into a narrow alley where the glow of orange lanterns flickered against the walls.

The place was old — the kind of joint where those who didn’t belong came to disappear.

Some hid from their pasts, some from the law, others from themselves.

Laughter, raucous and raw, spilled from inside.

A sign hung crooked above the door, the paint chipped and the name long unreadable.

## 🩸Fangs

Soot pushed through the entrance.

Warm, smoky air met her face. Tables cluttered the floor, and Pokémon lounged in mismatched chairs — a den of dark types, misfits, and drifters.

Some laughed too loudly. Others stared at nothing, swirling their cups like they were trying to find meaning in the foam.

She walked past them, head low, tail steady — until a shape stumbled into her path.

A Zangoose blocked her way, fur matted, eyes glazed from drink. His grin was crooked, teeth catching the lantern light.

> “Well now… what’s a lovely shadow like you doin’ all alone?” he slurred, swaggering closer. <br>
> “You look like trouble — my favorite kind.”

Soot didn’t even glance at him, just trying to get around.

> “Move.”

He laughed, leaning in until she could smell the rot of his breath. His claw reached out, blocking her way.

> “C’mon, don’t be cold. Bet I could—”

The claw brushed Soot's side. She turned her head, eyes like molten gold. Her voice dropped to a whisper sharp enough to cut glass.

> “Get off or I'll make your throat an open-floor plan.”

The laughter in his throat died. He froze — half from fear, half disbelief.

Then he stepped back, paws raised.

> “Alright, alright… sheesh. Didn’t mean nothin’, sweetheart.”

She pushed past him without another word. The nearby table turned their eyes away.

The room, for a moment, felt colder.

Soot walked on, the murmuring behind her fading into the haze.

> “Oi, Soot!” called a voice from the far corner. <br>
> “Get over here, you gloomy thing!”

## 🍻 Company

She turned.

Two other dark-types — a scruffy Mightyena and a sly Liepard — called her over.

Her usual company. Not friends, not really, just others who’d been cast aside by brighter Pokémon.

Common outcasts. Common wounds.

She joined them, sitting on a cushion before a low table fit for quadrupeds like them.

The Mightyena grinned.

> “Thought you weren’t comin’ tonight. Missed your delightful attitude.”

Soot smirked faintly.

> “Missed your awful breath, Fang.”

The Liepard chuckled, tail flicking lazily.

> “He’s not wrong though — you vanish for a day and he starts whining about it.”

> “Stuff it, Dart,” Fang growled, though his grin said otherwise.

The two of them laughed — too loud, too empty.

A server passed by, setting a small bowl before her filled with a bronze liquid that shimmered like molten metal.

It smelled harsh enough to sting her nose. She hadn’t ordered it aloud — she didn’t need to.

They knew her usual.

She lapped at it once. It burned. It fizzed.

She hated it — the sting, the sourness, the way it seared down her throat.

But she didn’t drink it for the taste.

It helped her forget — and it did that well enough.

## 💭 Dreaming

As her throat burned, her mind drifted.

She saw him again in her thoughts — that Eevee from the night before.

The way he’d looked at her like she wasn’t something evil, like she wasn’t something to fear.

His voice had been soft, uncertain, yet full of that impossible kindness she didn't think existed.

He’d talked about hope, and sung about someone who’d saved him.

She didn't understand it then, and she didn’t now.

She imagined what her own savior might look like —

a warrior, maybe.

Someone strong enough to burn this rotten world down and carry her far from it.

Someone who’d fight for her, not judge her.

Someone who’d make her believe again.

For a heartbeat, she could almost see him — his fur shining like light through smoke, eyes steady, standing tall.

Then she blinked — and the vision shattered.

No one like that existed. Not for her.

Still… she wanted to see him again.

**Joseph.**

Even if she didn’t know why.

## 🙊 Stories

Her companions were still talking — jokes, gossip, scraps of half-truths.

Dart leaned back with a smug grin.

> “So get this — I was at the market earlier, right? Some snobby Luxray starts braggin’ about how his claws are gold-plated or some crap. Says no one’s worth more than him.” <br>
> “So I _accidentally_ bumped him and made him drop his fancy collar into the gutter.”

Fang barked a laugh.

> “You didn’t!”

> “Oh, I did. You should’ve seen his face. Thought he was gonna skin me alive.”

Soot tested the drink again, more out of habit than want.

> “Would’ve been an improvement.”

They both laughed, and Dart shot her a playful glare.

> “Ha! Maybe. But hey, at least I made him remember that worth isn’t bought.”

> “Yeah, at least we know we're worthless,” Soot said flatly.

They howled with laughter again.

She forced a chuckle too — sharp and convincing — but her eyes stayed dull.

If only they knew how much she meant it.

The laughter died down and they continued talking. Their conversation drifted around her, meaningless hum and smoke —

until one word caught like a spark in dry grass.

> “—Eevee,”

## 💫 Impulse

Her head lifted before she realized it.

> “Wait,” she said. <br>
> “What Eevee?”

The Liepard shrugged.

> “Heard some new Eevee refused to bow at the procession or whatever. Caused a scene.”

> “Nah,” said Mightyena, <br>
> “he was caught talkin’ blasphemy or somethin’ stupid like that.”

> “Did he get hurt?” she pressed. <br>
> “Do you know his name?”

They both shrugged, bickering over details.

> “I heard he ran,” said Liepard.

> “Ran? Nah, they dragged him off!” said Mightyena.

> “You’re full of it.”

> “Am not! That’s what Rocks told me.”

Their voices blurred into static as her pulse quickened.

Was it **him**?

Joseph?

Why would he do something so reckless…?

Her chest tightened. The noise around her dulled until all she could hear was her own pulse.

If it was him — if he’d really been caught — what then?

She didn’t know where he was, only the direction he’d gone when they last parted.

That camp. Maybe he’d gone back there. Maybe he was fine.

She wanted to believe that. Needed to.

The idea of him hurt — dragged off or worse…

Her claws punctured the cloth of the pillow.

The thought of doing nothing made her sick.

She had to see for herself — to know.

Maybe she was a fool for it, but she’d rather be a fool than pretend she didn’t care.

She rose suddenly, the cushion shifting beneath her paws.

> “Where you goin’, Soot?” Fang called after her.

But she was already moving.

The lanterns blurred past. Her steps turned to strides — strides to sprints.

The cool night air bit at her fur as she burst through the door and into the open street.

The night outside felt colder now. The stars were still watching, unblinking.

**Alone.**

The wind chased her as she ran.

## 🏕️ Recklessness

Dry grass whispered beneath her paws, the town shrinking behind her until its orange lanterns became flickers swallowed by the dark.

The path ahead stretched thin and lonely beneath the moonlight — a thread of silver through sleeping fields.

She didn’t stop until she found the camp.

Just outside, she scoped the area:

five tents, a fire pit, a cart, boxes of supplies — ready to move at a moment’s notice.

A very temporary living arrangement.

She slowed, crouching low as she neared the tents. Her chest heaved.

Every instinct screamed this was foolish — reckless.

What would they do if they caught her? They could do whatever they wanted. Jail? Beat? Kill?

She shook the thought away.

She needed to know he was alright.

The place was quiet — no guards, no lights, just the hiss of wind through canvas.

She crept forward, paws silent.

> “Joseph?” she whispered. <br>
> “Joseph, you here?”

Nothing.

She moved further in, ears twitching at every rustle.

> “Joseph?” she tried again, louder.

Still no answer.

Her stomach twisted.

_This is idiotic,_ she thought. _If they wake up and find me here, I’m done for._

> She exhaled. “Maybe he’s not even—”

A sudden rustle froze her mid-sentence.

A low growl rippled through the dark.

> “Who’s there?”

## 🚨 Caught

The voice was cold and commanding.

Then, out of the shadows — pale gold fur caught the moonlight.

Nine tails flowing like living fire.

He stepped into view, eyes burning like coals.

> “An Umbreon,” he said, tone laced with disgust. <br>
> “Of course. What are you doing here, shadow?”

Soot stiffened.

> “None of your business.”

> “Wrong answer.” His voice was soft, dangerous. <br>
> “You think we wouldn’t notice you creeping through our camp at night? What are you — a thief? A spy?”

Shapes stirred.

Silhouettes emerged — a Tyrogue, a Lucario, a Pikachu.

The Tyrogue cracked his knuckles.

> “No one just _wanders_ into our camp at night.”

> “Probably scouting,” Snout added. <br>
> “Or worse.”

> “What do you want, intruder?” Chew demanded, cheeks sparking.

Their words crashed over her — too fast to fight back.

She bared her teeth, tail low but defiant.

> “Back off.”

The Ninetales’s tails lifted, eyes narrow.

> “Answer me. What are you doing here?”

Her jaw clenched.

> “I’m looking for someone.”

> “Oh?” he said.

> “Who could you possibly be looking for here?”

Her throat tightened.

> “Joseph.”

The crowd murmured — sharp, confused, incredulous.

> “You can’t have him!” cried the Pikachu.

Then —

> “Wait — what’s going on?”

## 🔥 Intercession

Everyone turned.

From the far side, a small figure pushed through the menagerie — brown fur tousled, eyes wide.

**Joseph.**

He stumbled forward, looking from the Ninetales to the Umbreon surrounded by others.

> “Soot?”

> “Joseph,” she breathed, relief and disbelief tangled in her voice.

Her tone blindsided him — warmth, worry — why would she care?

The Ninetales glanced at him.

> “You know this intruder?”

> “I— Yes,” Joseph stammered, moving between them. <br>
> “She’s with me.”

> “With you?” the Lucario scoffed. <br>
> “You made friends with _her_?”

> “Joseph, do you even know what she is?” the Pikachu cried. <br>
> “She’s an _Umbreon_!”

The word hit the air like poison.

Soot’s muscles tensed. She’d heard that tone all her life — fear hiding behind disgust.

Joseph turned toward the Pikachu.

> “I know what she is, and I don’t care!”

## 🕯️ Resolve

A murmur rippled through the group — disbelief, shock, outrage.

Eli’s tails flicked. Chew’s sparks dimmed.

Silence fell, heavy and loud.

Soot blinked at him, chest tightening.

For someone to say that — out loud, _for her_ — it didn’t make sense.

Her throat went dry. She wanted to scoff, to call him naïve — but the words wouldn’t come.

Joseph turned toward the Ninetales.

> “Please — just let me take her out of camp. I’ll make sure she doesn’t cause trouble.”

The Ninetales studied him for a long moment. Then finally —

> “Fine. But if anything happens, it’s on you.”

> “I understand,” Joseph nodded.

> “Come on. Follow me.”

The circle parted reluctantly. Every stare burned into her fur.

No one said another word — but the silence said plenty.

Once they passed beyond the tents, the noise of camp fell away.

Only the chirr of insects filled the dark.

Joseph led the way down a narrow trail until the trees opened into a clearing —

the lake spread before them, black and silver beneath the stars.

The moon hung low, its reflection rippling in the gentle waves.
