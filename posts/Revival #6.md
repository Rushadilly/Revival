Issue #6 of the short-story series — Revival, and oh boy! Things are really heating up this issue, and I mean that in **every** sense of the word! For real!

Things have been easy for Joseph up to this point, but he is about to see what this world thinks of people like him, and he won't be able to hide from it. 😁👍

---

### 🎪 Market

The town hummed as the sun climbed the horizon.

Dust shimmered in the air, catching light between canvas awnings and stone walls.

Laughter, pawsteps, and snatches of chatter spilled across the street, folding into a melody of morning commerce.

Voices bargained.

Merchants called.

Wagons rumbled.

The breeze carried the scent of spice, fruit, and baked goods — lively but small, the kind of town that managed to be both weary and warm.

Everyone knew everyone, and the unspoken rules of civility carried more weight than law ever did.

Joseph kept to the edge of the crowd, his pouches bumping against his sides as he followed Chew through the stalls.

The **Eevee’s** tail twitched at each noise, his steps careful on the dirt.

Ahead of him, **the Pikachu’s** satchel swung cheerily at his hip, and Chew hummed as if the day itself had been written for him.

> “Alright!”

Chew said, pulling a folded list from his bag.

> “Eli said we need bandages, berries, thread— This all seems simple enough.”

Joseph nodded, keeping his focus low.

> “Right.”

The earlier tension between them still hung in the air — what happened after the race, the way Joseph had growled without meaning to.

Neither had spoken of it since.

It wasn’t a wound, but it hadn’t healed either.

Chew glanced back, tail flicking.

> “Let’s just get what we need and head out. Shouldn’t take long.”

They moved down the lane together, weaving through the marketplace.

Stalls lined both sides — woven baskets piled with berries, stacks of rolled bandages, rows of gleaming trinkets carved from shell and stone.

Pokémon of all kinds bartered and passed by, tails flicking, scales glinting, feathers ruffling.

Joseph kept his ears low and his gaze lower.

The sound, the motion, the proximity — it all pressed in on him.

Every laugh felt too loud, every greeting too directed.

He focused on small things: counting coins, noting prices, pretending he belonged.

If he just blended in, maybe no one would notice how new he still was to this world.

But someone had noticed.

Across the lane, between two awnings, a **Sylveon** paused mid-conversation with a vendor.

Her ribbons swayed lazily, but her gaze lingered on Joseph — not harshly, just curious, as if cataloging something unfamiliar.

New faces were rare in towns like this.

After a heartbeat, she tilted her head before turning back to the vendor.

Joseph never saw her, but the moment stayed with her longer than she expected.

Then, Joseph and Chew came upon the shrine.

### 🔥 Sensation

It stood at the intersection where the dirt gave way to a widened cobblestone circle, surrounded by candles and weathered offerings.

A carved figure loomed at its center—a stylized beast of stone, wings curled like storm clouds, eyes set with dull amber.

Flowers and coins lined the steps.

Some Pokémon bowed before it, others passed without a glance.

Chew’s ears perked.

> “Hey! It’s a shrine to **Moltres**. I didn’t know they had one here.”

Joseph didn’t answer.

> “You should make an offering,” Chew said, his voice bright.
> <br>“You know, just to be safe.”

> “I’ll pass.”

Chew tilted his head.

> “C’mon, it can’t hurt.”

But Joseph didn’t meet his eyes.

He didn’t say why—didn’t say that to him, it _did_ hurt.

Bowing before carved wings felt like betrayal.

He already had a God, and he wasn't about to turn his back on Him.

> “Suit yourself,”

Chew said, shrugging as he trotted up to the shrine.

He pulled a few berries from his satchel and placed them before the statue.

> “For good luck.”

Joseph followed behind, ears off to the side.

As he did this sensation grew—**a shimmer**,

faint at first, like sunlight through glass.

Stronger as he approached, heat seeped **past** his fur.

_Searing,_ though no flame touched him.

He felt it prickling his skin with… something like sparkles that _sharpened_ as he neared the shrine.

It became so intense that he had to step away.

He turned back at the shrine from a distance.

It looked like mere stone.

Chew finished his offering and returned to Joseph.

> “Hey,” Chew said,
> <br>“you okay?”

Joseph blinked, his gaze never leaving the shrine.

> “Yeah, I'm fine… Did you feel anything strange, like heat?”

> “Heat? Nah. It’s just the sun. You sure you’re not getting sick or something?”

> “No… no I'm fine — I must have been… imagining it.”

But even as they walked away, he couldn’t stop running through explanations in his head.

Radiation? No, ridiculous—at that intensity everyone in town would be dead or dying.

Just heat from the candles?

Then why did no one else react?

The logic didn’t hold, and it left him uneasy.

### 🙇‍♂️ Defiance

They continued their errands in silence.

Markets bustled, carts rattled, sunlight crawled higher up the rooftops.

Eventually, their bags began to fill—berries, bandages, thread, a handful of new rations.

Then, the noise around them faltered.

A ripple of stillness swept through the street.

The laughter, the bargaining, the rhythm of paws and hooves—it all dimmed as if a single breath had been drawn and held.

One by one, Pokémon turned toward the square.

A **procession** was passing through.

At its center, several cloaked figures carried a long platform draped in silks of red and gold.

Upon it rested an ornate vessel of stone and metal, etched with old runes that pulsed faintly with light.

The figures surrounding it—an Arcanine, a Gardevoir, a Noctowl, a Lucario—all cloaked holding staffs, standing ahead and behind, keeping the path clear and the vessel protected.

> “Bow,” someone whispered beside them.
> <br>“The blessing begins.”

Without hesitation, those around them lowered themselves to the ground, heads bent low.

The silence deepened.

Chew dropped immediately, pressing his forehead close to the stones.

He looked up just long enough to hiss,

> “Joseph! You have to bow!”

Joseph stood frozen.

His muscles screamed at him to move.

Everyone else was kneeling—dozens of them—and still, he couldn’t.

His heart raced; fear crawled up his spine.

_Just do it,_ a part of him pleaded.

_Blend in, don’t make a scene._

But another voice whispered below them: _Don’t._

**He didn’t bow.**

The **Lucario’s** hood tilted toward him.

Though his eyes were hidden, Joseph felt their focus like a hand closing around his throat.

> “You there,” came the voice — calm, but edged like flint.
> <br>“Why do you not kneel before the blessing?”

Joseph’s throat closed.

He tried to speak, but no sound came.

> “I asked you a question,” the Lucario said, stepping closer.

The staff tapped once against the stone.

> “Why do you defy the sacred act?”

Joseph’s breath came quick.

He searched for something to say—any excuse that wouldn’t end with him being struck down.

His mind screamed for a lie.

But something else spoke first, quieter, clearer.

_Just tell the truth._

> “My- I-I'm sorry… My- my God won’t allow me to bow.” his voice trembled out.

The Lucario froze.

For a long heartbeat, neither moved.

Then, slowly, he lifted his staff high.

Joseph flinched.

His body’s first instinct was to cower onto the ground—to escape the swing.

But for some reason that didn’t happen.

He merely turned his head aside, eyes shut tight, braced for the blow.

Then—**wind**.

### 💨 Breath

A single gust swept the street.

It wasn’t strong, but it carried a depth that stole every sound.

Tarps snapped,

banners rippled,

coins rattled,

and the flames of the shrine flickered backward without smoke.

For a heartbeat, every living thing seemed to hold still—caught in something far larger than air.

Then it passed, soft as a sigh.

When Joseph opened his eyes, the Lucario had lowered his staff, maw lightly agape.

The hood still hid his eyes, but his stance had shifted—no longer ready to strike.

Without a word, he turned and rejoined the procession.

Slowly, one by one, the bowed Pokémon lifted their heads.

Movement rippled through the crowd, confused and uneven.

No one spoke at first—just small, sharp breaths and the rustle of fabric as they rose.

Then the whispers began.

> “Did you see that?”

Joseph's ears twitched before he even caught the words.

> “Was that the shrine’s blessing—or wrath?”

Another flick—unbidden, sharp. Every sound pulled at him.

> “Is he cursed?”

He tried to still them. They kept moving. Scanning for danger.

> “No, protected. Didn’t you feel it? Something _moved._”

Joseph forced his ears against his skull.

He just wanted the chaotic chatter to stop.

Still, some glanced at Joseph with suspicion, others with awe, but all with unease.

Children peeked out from behind parents’ legs.

A merchant made the sign of warding with trembling paws.

Another shuffled her wares closer as though afraid the air itself might strike next.

Joseph stood in the middle of it all, heart hammering.

The crowd parted just slightly around him—like a current avoiding an obstacle too dangerous to touch.

Chew rose beside him, wide-eyed.

> “I- I thought you were a goner,” he whispered.
> <br>“He was gonna _hit_ you.”

His voice trembled, ears dropped low.

Joseph didn’t look around.

He could still feel their gazes clinging to him, heavy and uncertain.

The world suddenly seemed too bright.

He leaned close to Chew for help,

> “C-can we go somewhere they didn’t see that?”

Chew nodded quickly.

They slipped away down the nearest side street, the murmurs following like a tide that wouldn’t let go.

### 🔮 Crystal

They didn’t stop walking until the voices were gone.

Joseph’s heart hadn’t slowed; every beat still thudded with the echo of the Lucario’s staff striking stone.

The murmurs had chased them through the alleys — whispers of _the one who didn’t bow,_ and _the wind that answered._

Now the town’s noise was softer, a few streets away from the market’s heart.

A fountain trickled nearby, the sound fragile compared to the memory of that storm.

Joseph's chest heaved as he dropped his head, pressing his face into the dirt.

Chew finally spoke, voice low.

> “They’re still talking about you, you know. Half the town saw it.”

> “Yeah. I noticed.”

> “You think they’ll—?”

> “I don’t know!”

He lifted his head and started pacing.

> “Maybe… maybe they’ll just forget? People forget things fast.”

Chew frowned.

> “That? I don’t think they’re gonna forget that.”

Silence fell between them.

A gust tugged at Joseph’s fur — gentle this time, ordinary.

He dropped his head again and exhaled, long and shaky.

The weight of everything pressed in: the fear, the stares, the miracle he felt like wasn’t supposed to happen.

When he finally lifted his head, he realized someone was watching them.

Across the street, half-hidden by a row of market tents, stood a **Sylveon.**

Her ribbons swayed lazily in the breeze, catching the light.

She wasn’t pretending not to stare — her gaze was direct, sharp, almost curious.

Joseph blinked.

> “Do… do you know her?”

Chew followed his glance.

> “Nope. But I think she knows _you._”

The Sylveon tilted her head, then began walking their way.

Her steps were deliberate, light, graceful — not cautious, but confident, like she already knew how this conversation would end.

Joseph felt his stomach tighten. _Great._

He wasn’t ready for another confrontation — not now.

When she stopped before them, her smile was polite, but her eyes flickered with something else.

> “You’re the one from the square,” she said softly.
> <br>“The one who didn’t bow.”

Joseph tensed.

> “That— I don’t want trouble.”

> “Trouble?” She laughed — musical, quick.

> “No, no. Quite the opposite.”

Her ribbons fluttered closer, a gesture both curious and teasing.

> “That was brave. Foolish, maybe, but brave. Everyone else dropped like frightened Meowths. But you—” her smile sharpened—
> <br>“you stayed standing.”

Chew scratched behind his ear, unsure whether to grin or back away.

> “I just… I couldn’t-”

Joseph said, his voice awkward and defensive.

> “I believe you.” She stepped closer.
> <br>“My name’s **Crystal.** And you, little hero?”

> “Joseph.”

> “Joseph,” she repeated, like testing how the word felt on her tongue.
> <br>“You wear it well.”

The compliment hit him off guard.

Joseph blinked.

> “Uh— thanks?”

He turned his head back, charting his exit plan.

> “You’re kind of cute when you’re nervous.”

Joseph’s cheeks flushed under his fur.

> “C-cute?”

Her smile deepened.

> “Mm-hm.”

Then, with a lazy flick of her ribbons, her laugh came again, soft and playful.

> “Relax. I don’t bite.”

A pause.

> “Not unless asked.”

Joseph's ears dropped and his eyes dilated.

Chew blinked between them, puzzled.

> “Wait—what’s that supposed to mean?”

> **“NOTHING!””** Joseph blurted out, already turning away.
> <br>“We should go.”

> “Oh?” the Sylveon retorted.

Her ribbons curled back, a touch of mock disappointment.

> “And here I thought you were fearless.”

> “I'm sorry — you were wrong.”

The smile slipped for a heartbeat — surprise flickering across her face.

> “Wrong?” She echoed, voice quieter now.

Joseph didn’t answer, already walking away.

> “Maybe,” she murmured.
> <br>“But I’d still like to know what made you stand.”

He didn’t look back.

Behind him, she whispered almost to herself.

> “No one else did.”

### 🚬 Embers

Chew trailed after him, snickering under his breath.

> “Sooo… new friend?”

Joseph didn’t answer.

If he ignored it, maybe it would just die off.

He adjusted the strap of his pouch and kept walking, eyes fixed on the dust scrolling under his paws.

> “She called you _cute!_” Chew went on, grinning ear to ear.
> <br>“That’s—uh, not something I’ve heard anyone say in a while.”

Joseph’s ears pinned back.

> “You want her t—tu-t-cu- ye–ca—”

He groaned, shaking his head.

> “You want her to cu–c–tu-c–yu-ca–tu— HECK THIS STUPID FLINGING FAILURE OF A TONGUE!”

Chew blinked, then burst out laughing.

> “You okay there, buddy?”

Joseph huffed, glaring at the ground.

> “I was trying to say — _you want her to call_ **_you_** _cute next time?_”

Chew snorted, tail flicking.

> “Oh. Well, no, I don’t.”

> “Yeah, I didn’t think so,” Joseph muttered, tone flat and deliberate.

They walked in silence for a while.

Chew’s chuckles faded to the occasional snort, while Joseph focused on steadying his breathing.

He could still feel the tremor in his voice, the way his tongue had tied itself in knots.

Every replay of it made his ears burn hotter. _Real smooth,_ he thought.

Chew finally spoke up, grin audible in his voice.

> “You sure you’re alright? Looked like your brain just shorted out back there.”

Joseph gave a small exhale that might have been a laugh.

> “I'm fine… Just — trying not to combust.”

> “Hey, for what it’s worth—you handled the whole thing back there better than I would’ve.”

Joseph hesitated, the unexpected sincerity cooling the heat in his cheeks.

> “Thanks,” he said finally.
> <br>“I think.”

Just as he was about to regain his composure, Joseph glanced over his shoulder—

A strange feeling prickling at the back of his neck.

Down the street, a shimmer of **pink ribbons** slipped out of view behind a stall — gone before he could be sure she’d been there at all.
